Kitchen Water Damage in Santa Rosa

Count the dishwasher, disposal, drain, faucet and fridge line, and the kitchen has more places for water to connect than anywhere else in the house. Most of these connections are tucked into cabinets or hidden at the back of appliances. If one starts leaking, water heads for the back of the base cabinet and works its way under the floor. We help Santa Rosa homeowners find it, dry it, and save as much of the kitchen as possible.

The costly piece of a kitchen leak is the cabinets. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. We pull toe kicks and drill small hidden holes to move dry air under and behind the cabinets, so in many kitchens the cabinets and countertops stay right where they are while everything dries.

What It Looks Like

  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • A brown stain on the ceiling of the room below the kitchen
  • Warping, staining or a soft spot in the floor of the cabinet below the sink
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • A damp, stale smell when you open the cabinet under the sink
Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Under the Base Cabinets

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. It gathers water that sits for weeks, and the resulting mold stays hidden unless the cabinets are removed.

Behind the Dishwasher

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. None of it is visible behind the appliance until the flooring starts to change.

Refrigerator Water Line

A few drops per minute can escape from the narrow tube that supplies the ice maker. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

The Wall Behind the Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. When they leak, framing and drywall get wet behind the cabinets, and mold spreads where no one can see it.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. It slips beneath flooring through the seams and edges.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Particleboard cabinet bases and toe kicks soak up water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

Laminate swells at the edges, hardwood cups, and vinyl lifts once the adhesive under it fails. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

Past one week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. The subfloor may be coming apart in layers, and mold behind cabinets often means they come out together with the countertop.

How We Fix It

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Stop the Source

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Measure the Wet Area

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. For several days the dehumidifiers and air movers keep running, and damp cavities receive an antimicrobial treatment.

4

Remove Only Damage

We carefully remove any flooring, subfloor or cabinet section that cannot be saved. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.
