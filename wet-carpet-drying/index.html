Wet Carpet Drying: What Santa Rosa Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. For Santa Rosa homes, our crew handles the extraction, meters the carpet, pad and subfloor, and runs drying gear that gets to the bottom layer.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. With an appliance leak, which is gray water, we often discard the pad, then clean and treat the carpet. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • A rust colored line at the wall edge, a sign of wet tack strip
  • Musty, sour odor that builds whenever the door and windows stay shut
  • Days after the water was cleaned up, the carpet is cool and clammy to the touch

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. The odor arrives a long time ahead of any visible sign on top.

Clean Water Turns Dirty

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. Soil and bacteria build up in it inside the carpet and pad, and a carpet that was dryable can end up being torn out.

Stains From Furniture

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Damp Base of the Walls

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. Drywall and baseboards wick it upward, and nobody notices because the carpet covers the edge.

How We Fix It

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. The water category gets identified as well, since it determines what we can keep.

2

Extract the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

The first few hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. The subfloor swells and the tack strip nails begin to rust.

After a week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. The work gets bigger, since mold is often on the subfloor and the lower edge of the drywall.
