Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. Our Santa Rosa crew mitigates a loss in four moves: control the source, extract the water, contain the wet zone and stabilize the building. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. Each is its own phase of the job. Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the loss, limit the spread, stabilize the home. Afterward comes restoration, when dryness is verified and ruined flooring, drywall and finishes get fixed or rebuilt. Because an insurer lists each one separately as well, knowing where you stand in the process is helpful.

On a standard mitigation call we work through four items. First is source control, which keeps more water from coming in. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Fourth is stabilization: unsalvageable materials come out, surfaces receive antimicrobial treatment, and dehumidifiers and air movers are placed. Furniture and personal items are protected too, either relocated or raised on blocks.

Signs You Need Water Damage Mitigation in Santa Rosa

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
  • Bathroom or kitchen cabinets are sitting over water that cannot be reached to dry.
  • The insurance company instructed you to take reasonable action against further damage.
  • Since the leak you have noticed puffed-up baseboards or gaps opening behind them.
  • An opening in the roof or at a window lets rainwater enter the building.
  • One day on, damp materials are beginning to smell musty.
  • The carpet pad is soaked through even where the surface seems only damp.

What a Professional Brings to Water Damage Mitigation

Doing mitigation well means making a string of quick judgment calls. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Which category applies, 1, 2 or 3, and did that change after the leak first began? Should the toe kick under the cabinets be pulled? Our team makes these calls from training and from moisture readings, not from how a surface looks or feels to the hand.

There is a claim-related reason to do mitigation right as well. Under most policies you must act reasonably to keep the damage from growing, with proof that you did. We photograph conditions on arrival, record moisture readings daily and keep a list of anything removed. That file shows what the water did and what was done about it. Whoever handles repairs then begins from a known condition, and nothing wet ends up sealed behind new wallboard.

Our Water Damage Mitigation Process

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Control the Source

Depending on the cause, our crew closes the supply, caps a failed line, or covers the entry point with tarps and boards. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Extract the Water

Standing water is removed with pumps and extractors, and carpet and pad are checked. Drying machines on their own cannot limit damage the way prompt bulk extraction does.

3

Contain Wet Areas

We isolate the affected rooms from the rest through airflow control and plastic barriers. That way dust, humidity and contaminants do not move through the home during the work.

4

Stabilize and Dry

Unsalvageable materials are removed, surfaces get antimicrobial treatment, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set. We log moisture readings each visit until the structure is ready for repairs, usually within 3 to 5 days.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Trade rubber washer hoses for braided steel, and renew them roughly every five years.
  • A main shutoff that will not turn is no help, so locate and test yours.
  • Never switch the furnace fully off in freezing weather, since pipes can freeze and split.
  • Check under sinks and around toilets monthly for drips, stains or soft flooring.
  • Clean the gutters regularly, and aim downspouts so they discharge away from the foundation.
  • Have your water heater inspected yearly and replace it before the tank fails.
