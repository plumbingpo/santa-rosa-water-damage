Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Santa Rosa

Sump pumps do a single task, yet they commonly quit right when that task matters. During heavy rain the water table climbs and fills the pit, at which point an outage, a stuck float or an overwhelmed pump stops the pumping. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

Clean is the wrong word for what comes out of a sump pit. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

First Five Minutes

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stay Out of the WaterDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Outlets, the pump cord or the furnace might already be wet and live.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. If the panel is in the water, call the utility.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyA tripped outlet, a loose plug or a float wedged against the basin wall are the things to look for. Do not reach into the water.
  4. Treat the Water as ContaminatedIf going down cannot be avoided, put on gloves and rubber boots, leave pets and children upstairs, and wash up after handling wet items.
  5. Move Valuables and CallTake pictures of the pump and the water level, bring valuables that are still dry upstairs, and phone us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Check valve trouble, which makes the pump move the same water again and again.
  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.
  • Motor failure in a pump that has simply reached the end of its life.
  • Outside the house, a crushed, clogged or separated discharge pipe.
  • A float switch that is stuck, tangled or pinned against the basin wall.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Carpet and Flooring

There is no safe way to clean pad and carpet that sat in groundwater, so both are removed. Laminate and vinyl plank trap silty water underneath and usually come up too.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

Sump pits tend to be located near the home's mechanical equipment. Controls, wiring and burners can be destroyed by water, so every appliance needs an inspection before it runs again.

Bottom Plates and Studs

Bottom plates rest directly on concrete, so they are the first framing to get wet. We clean, disinfect and dry the studs and plates after removing the wet drywall.

Finished Basement Walls

The wet zone inside a wall reaches well past the water mark, as drywall and insulation pull moisture up off the floor. The lower part that got wet is cut away, not dried, on account of the contamination.

Will Insurance Pay?

Does Insurance Cover It?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. Water backing up or overflowing from a sump is usually excluded under a standard homeowners policy, and a flood policy is a different product with rules of its own. Often the only coverage comes from an added endorsement for water backup and sump overflow, which has limits of its own. Check the declarations page for it, or call your agent to find out. We record the pump failure, the water level and the damage so your claim file is complete.
